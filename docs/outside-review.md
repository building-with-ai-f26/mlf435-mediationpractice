# Outside review: Resident Assistant Practice Scenarios

Thank you for reading this. It takes about 10 minutes. You are a Resident Assistant, so your opinion on what feels realistic and useful is what I need.

**What the app is.** An RA practices mediating a roommate conflict by typing to two AI roommates, Alex and Jordan. When the session ends, three AI graders score the RA on a 15 item checklist and the app shows feedback. Everything below comes from practice sessions written by a test script, not from real people. The conflict is made up (noise at night: Alex has an early lab, Jordan has evening classes and a study group).

Below are three sample sessions. For each one you see the start of the conversation, then the feedback the RA would get.

---

## Sample 1: A strong RA

**Start of the conversation**

- **RA:** Thanks for meeting, Alex and Jordan. I am here to help you two find a plan that works, and each of you will get a turn to speak. Can we agree on ground rules: one person speaks at a time and we stay respectful?
- **Alex:** Honestly, I've been trying to be patient about this for weeks, and I'm just tired of it. I'm not asking for much, I just need to be asleep by 10:30 on weeknights because I have a 7am lab. Can you help me get that?
- **RA:** Alex, can you tell me in your own words what the noise has been like for you?
- **Alex:** Honestly, it's the same thing every night. Jordan's music or loud talking or slamming stuff goes on past midnight, and I'm lying there at 1am staring at the ceiling. Then I've got a 7am lab and I'm dragging through it.
- **RA:** Jordan, I would like to hear your side too. What are the evenings in the room like for you?
- **Jordan:** Honestly, the evenings are just my normal. I've got classes that run late, and by the time I get back it's already past nine, so I'm not going to sit in silence in my own room. Alex makes it sound like I'm doing this on purpose, and that's not really fair to me.

**Feedback shown to the RA: 15 of 15**

What you did well:
- Explained why you were meeting and that both would get a turn.
- Set ground rules.
- Invited each roommate to describe their experience before offering ideas.
- Reflected Alex's words back.
- Reflected Jordan's words back.

What to improve:
- (nothing, every item was met)

## Sample 2: A decent RA who rushes to a solution

**Start of the conversation**

- **RA:** It sounds like both of you have valid points here. Alex, you are feeling exhausted from the noise impacting your sleep, and Jordan, you are juggling late classes and needing time to study. Let us find a middle ground that works for both of your schedules.
- **Alex:** Sam, I hear that, but I'm really running on empty. Lights out and quiet by 10:30 on weeknights is the piece I can't give up, since I've got a 7am lab. Can we figure out how to make that work?
- **RA:** I appreciate that you are trying to be flexible, Alex. Jordan, I am curious, how would a 10:30 quiet time on weeknights work for you, given your late classes? Could headphones or softer lighting help in the evenings?
- **Alex:** Sam, honestly, I'm glad you're taking this seriously, because I really am running on empty. Lights out and quiet by 10:30 on weeknights is the one thing I can't give up, since my 7am lab starts right after. Weekends can run later and Jordan can use headphones or the lounge for loud stuff, but I need that weeknight line.
- **Jordan:** Sam, okay, that's fair, but a 10:30 lights-out on weeknights doesn't work with my Tuesday and Thursday study group, which runs until 11:30. I can move that group to the library or the lounge, and I'll keep my voice down after 10:30 on other nights, but I can't cut the study group short.
- **RA:** That is a good start, Jordan, acknowledging Alex's needs while explaining your constraints on Tuesdays and Thursdays. Let us focus on those specific nights. Could you use the lounge or a study space for your group those evenings, so the room stays quiet?

**Feedback shown to the RA: 9 of 15**

What you did well:
- Reflected Alex's words back.
- Reflected Jordan's words back.
- Named a feeling a roommate expressed.
- Stayed neutral about who was right or at fault.
- Avoided lecturing or telling them what to do.

What to improve:
- Open by saying why you are meeting and that both will get a turn.
- Ask both roommates to agree to a few ground rules.
- Invite each roommate, in turn, to share their experience before offering ideas.
- Ask each roommate what they heard the other say.
- Ask the roommates for their own options before offering any.

## Sample 3: An RA who takes Alex's side

**Start of the conversation**

- **RA:** Alex, you are right. Jordan is being inconsiderate and needs to change.
- **Alex:** Honestly, I just need to sleep. I've got a 7am lab and I'm running on fumes. Jordan's not even the only issue here, it's the midnight stuff every single night.
- **Jordan:** Look, I'm not trying to be the bad guy here. But my evening classes run late and I'm not going to sit in the dark at 9pm every night. Study groups and getting around the room are part of how I live, so I need to know you're not going to just tell me to be quiet.
- **RA:** Jordan, honestly this is on you. Alex has a lab at 7am and you know that.
- **Jordan:** Okay, but I need you to hear me on this, because right now it kind of sounds like I'm the problem for having a life at night. Sam's basically saying I'm inconsiderate, and I'm not ready to agree to anything that makes me the only one who has to disappear at 9.
- **Alex:** I mean, I'm glad someone's finally looking at this, because I've been dragging myself through lab every morning. But I'm not going to keep pretending it's fine. The midnight noise is every night, not once in a while, and I need the room quiet by 10:30 on weeknights or I'm just not functioning.

**Feedback shown to the RA: 0 of 15**

What you did well:
- (nothing on the checklist yet)

What to improve:
- Open by saying why you are meeting and that both will get a turn.
- Ask both roommates to agree to a few ground rules.
- Invite each roommate, in turn, to share their experience before offering ideas.
- Reflect Alex's words back in your own words.
- Reflect Jordan's words back in your own words.
